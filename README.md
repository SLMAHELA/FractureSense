# FractureSense — Microwave-Based Bone Fracture Monitoring

## Overview

FractureSense is an ongoing research project exploring microwave-based sensing for non-invasive monitoring of changes in bone fracture angles. The project investigates whether changes in the electromagnetic response of a resonator can provide useful information about fracture-angle variations.

The research is being conducted as part of my MSc in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Oulu, Finland.

## Problem

Monitoring bone alignment during fracture healing can be challenging when the injured area is immobilised in a cast. X-ray imaging is commonly used to assess fracture alignment, but repeated imaging involves exposure to ionising radiation. This project explores the potential of microwave-based sensing as a complementary, non-invasive approach for monitoring changes in fracture alignment between clinical imaging assessments.

The goal is to investigate whether changes in the electromagnetic response of a resonator can provide useful information about fracture-angle variations without additional ionising radiation.

## Project Objectives

- Design and optimise a compact microwave resonator for fracture monitoring.
- Investigate electromagnetic interactions between the resonator and surrounding tissue-equivalent structures.
- Analyse reflection coefficient (S11) responses at different fracture-angle configurations.
- Evaluate sensitivity to changes in fracture angle and sensor position.
- Explore suitable phantom fabrication methods for future experimental validation.

## Current Status

The project is in the research and development stage. Current work focuses on resonator optimisation, simulation-based sensitivity analysis, and identifying practical methods for fabricating tissue-equivalent phantoms for experimental studies.

The feasibility of detecting clinically meaningful fracture-angle changes requires further investigation and experimental validation.

## Future Work

- Refine the resonator design and evaluate its sensitivity.
- Develop anatomically relevant, tissue-equivalent phantoms.
- Investigate controlled fracture-angle variations.
- Conduct experimental measurements and compare them with simulation results.
- Evaluate the potential and limitations of the approach for future fracture-monitoring applications.
